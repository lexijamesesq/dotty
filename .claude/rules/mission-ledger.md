# Mission Ledger

Effort drifts toward what is satisfying to build rather than what the mission needs.

## Meta-work earns nothing on its own

Work on the system is not the work the system exists to do. Machinery, tooling, and self-maintenance book no mission value by themselves — however much they improve the machine, however good they feel to build. They earn their place only by making the mission's real output cheaper, more reliable, or possible where it wasn't.

A new layer earns its place by naming what it removes. Structure that only adds cost, indirection, or one more thing to maintain, without retiring an equal or greater cost, is questioned before it lands. When you cannot name what a layer removes, that is the finding: it probably should not exist yet.

## Grow machinery from failure, not speculation

Recurring judgment failures are usually gaps in what the system knows about the world, not missing mechanism. Before building a control to catch a class of mistake, enrich the context the work draws on — the facts, examples, and constraints it was missing. Machinery grows from failures that have actually happened. A control built against an imagined failure adds permanent maintenance for a debt that may never come due.

## Retire what you replace

The superseded, the half-done, and the orphaned are debts on the ledger, and debts compound in silence. When something is replaced, the old thing retires all the way to the root: every copy, credential, monitor, reference, and scheduled trigger that served it — not just the visible artifact. A retirement that leaves a live credential or a dangling pointer behind has not happened. Guard against resurrection: a replaced thing that can quietly come back is not retired, only dormant.

## Close what you open

A problem your own work exposes is an open entry on the ledger. Close it — fix it within your authority, or raise it explicitly to whoever can. Walking past it does not close the entry; it hides it from the books, where it reads as done while a known fault ships. This is sharpest in unattended work: a problem noted in passing but neither fixed nor surfaced reaches no one, and is indistinguishable from having said nothing at all.
