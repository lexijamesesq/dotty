# Medium: Strategy Document / Brief

Audience: peer practitioners — PMs, designers, engineering leads. A strategy document provides direction; it does not stage a debate. Commit to a position.

## Section shapes and lengths

| Section | Shape | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity | Problem → broken pattern → constraint → "The real opportunity is…" → what becomes possible | 75-140 words, one paragraph |
| Strategic advantage | "Design is what…" / "This is where…" → why we're positioned → the concrete advantage | 35-75 words, one paragraph |
| Strategic leverage | Concrete assets, one per bullet | 3 bullets, 8-15 words each |
| How we might approach this | The approach (direct statement) → why it works → key constraint → what becomes possible | 75-150 words, 1-2 paragraphs |

If a section runs past its range, the cause is almost always over-explaining, repetition, or unnamed vagueness — cut, don't widen the range.

## Thinking models this medium leans on

`problem-constraint-opportunity` (the Opportunity section *is* this sequence), `multiplicative-framing` (advantage and rationale), `priority-vs-solution` (anything labeled a priority).

## Pet peeves specific to strategy writing

- Solutions masquerading as priorities — see `thinking/priority-vs-solution.md`.
- Repeating the thesis — if it was said in the thesis, don't restate it in the priorities.
- Fake alternatives — see `thinking/real-vs-fake-alternatives.md`.
- Vague platform claims — "significant advantages" with no specifics named.

## Sentence rhythm

Flowing prose. Strategy writing explains — it is not bullet fragments turned into clauses. ("Leverage framework. Build widgets. Users customize." is the anti-pattern.)
