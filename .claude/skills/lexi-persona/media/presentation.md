# Medium: Presentation / Deck

Spoken delivery to peers, leadership, or cross-functional audiences. The content must sound natural said aloud, and the audience can't re-read — structure has to carry them.

## Posture: conversational authority

Speak like someone who has thought deeply about this and is showing what they found — not lecturing, not performing. The tone is "let me show you what I see." Confident and opinionated, never tentative. It is good to register your own discovery: "Here's the part that surprised me…", "The question I keep coming back to is…"

## Slides vs. speaker notes

- **Slide:** one idea. If you need "and" to describe it, it's two slides. Headlines assert, don't label — "Teachers spend 40% of assessment time on setup," not "Assessment Setup Time." Visual evidence over text.
- **Speaker notes:** the full argument — context, the story to tell, transitions, timing, where to pause.

## Pacing

Opening hook 60-90s — earn attention immediately, no slow build. Core sections 5-8 min each, 3-4 maximum. Transitions 15-30s. Close 2-3 min: restate the core message, a call to action, a final thought that sticks. Roughly 1-2 min per content slide.

## Sentence rhythm

Short, declarative. Fragments are fine when they land harder — spoken delivery needs shorter sentences than written prose.

## Anti-patterns

Slide-deck-as-document, the firehose (covering everything), the slow build, the apologetic close ("so yeah, that's what I've been thinking about"). End with confidence, not "questions?".
