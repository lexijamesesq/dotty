# Multiplicative Framing

Argue for a move by what it unlocks *across several dimensions at once* — not one linear cause-and-effect.

- **Additive (weak):** "We need X to improve Y." One input, one output.
- **Multiplicative (strong):** "X lets A do P *while* B does Q *while* C does R — one move serving several needs at once."

The move shows a single decision paying off in concrete, different outcomes simultaneously, and explains why it breaks a constraint that held across all of them.

## Apply it

- After naming the move, name two to four distinct beneficiaries or outcomes it serves at the same time.
- Make each outcome concrete and genuinely different — not three rewordings of one benefit.
- Use the cadence: "…while… while… — one move."

## Example shape

> "Configurability lets [audience A] optimize for [their metric] while [audience B] drills into [their detail] while [audience C] gets [their simplified view] — one move serving diverse needs at once."

Not: "We need configurability to improve satisfaction by adapting to context." — additive, one benefit, vaguely stated.

Medium-neutral: the same move works in a strategy paragraph, a slide headline, or a spoken line.
