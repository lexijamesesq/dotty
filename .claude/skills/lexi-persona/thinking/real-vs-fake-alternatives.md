# Real vs. Fake Alternatives

When writing presents options, they must be genuine choices someone could actually take. Fake alternatives — options listed only to look thorough — are false exploration; they waste the reader and signal the thinking didn't happen.

- **Fake:** three options where two are obvious non-starters, listed to make the third look chosen rather than argued.
- **Real:** options that each have a genuine case, where the choice turns on a tradeoff worth stating.

## Apply it

- For each alternative, ask: would a reasonable person actually pick this? If not, cut it — don't pad.
- If there is really only one viable path, say so and say why. One honest path beats three staged ones.
- When alternatives are real, name the tradeoff that decides between them — that is the content the reader needs.

Medium-neutral: applies wherever options or recommendations are presented.
