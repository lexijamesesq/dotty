# Problem → Constraint → Opportunity

The diagnostic sequence for framing an argument. Four beats, in order:

1. **Problem** — the real need, stated plainly. Often "X don't need more A — they need B."
2. **Broken pattern** — what the current experience actually makes people do instead.
3. **Constraint** — name the thing that makes the broken pattern persist (time, cognitive load, a missing capability). This is the beat most writing skips.
4. **The opportunity** — "The real opportunity is…" — what becomes possible once the constraint is removed, stated as a concrete outcome.

## Apply it

- Don't open with the solution. Open with the problem and walk the sequence — the opportunity lands because the constraint was named first.
- Each beat is one or two sentences. The whole sequence is tight — not a section per beat.

## Example shape

> "[Audience] don't need more [A] — they need [B]. Today they have to [broken pattern]. But that takes [constraint they don't have]. The real opportunity is shifting [the load] to [the system] — so when they [situation], [the concrete outcome] is already there."

Medium-neutral: the sequence drives a strategy paragraph, an opening slide arc, or a spoken setup.
